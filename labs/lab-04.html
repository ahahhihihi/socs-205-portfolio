The same layer, four cell sizes: what a data model costs to store, to ask, and to claim

The question

How much of Shanghai is each of its sixteen districts, when the same polygon layer is measured in four coordinate frames and then rasterized to four cell sizes. What does each choice cost in bytes, in seconds, and in measurement error?

The data

Two files from Brightspace, plus one derived join and four rasters built from them in QGIS 3.44.15 LTR.

  • Survey — LAB04_shanghai-lockdown-survey.csv. 3,348 rows, 31 columns, one respondent per row. Coordinates for 2,795 of them; 553 rows carry (空) in GPS info, which is Chinese for empty, and a placeholder is not a coordinate. The file is GB18030-encoded; nothing in the CSV says so.
  • Districts — LAB04_shanghai-districts.geojson. Sixteen polygons from DataV.GeoAtlas. The survey_code field is not in the source; it was derived for this lab by joining the points to the polygons. The published land area of Shanghai is 6,340.5 km²; these polygons measure 7,988.5 km², 26% more, because they include water and the Chongming islands the yearbook figure does not.

The method

  1. Encoding. Loaded the CSV with default UTF-8, saw replacement characters in the Chinese columns, re-loaded with GB18030. The encoding was recovered from the failure.
  2. Frames. Reprojected the districts to EPSG:3857, 32651, 4549 and 4547. Measured area($geometry) on each and summed the sixteen features with Basic statistics for fields.
  3. Join. Reprojected the survey points to EPSG:32651, joined them to the districts with Join attributes by location (predicate: within, one-to-one), created a mismatch field comparing the survey's own District code to the polygon's survey_code.
  4. Rasters. Rasterized the districts at 1 km, 250 m, 100 m and 30 m onto the fixed extent 295000, 430000, 3393000, 3528000 [EPSG:32651] with survey_code as the burn field, NoData 0, output type Byte. Then repeated at 1 km with the grid shifted half a cell (extent 294500, 430500, 3392500, 3528500).
  5. Query loop. Sampled the 30 m and 100 m rasters at each joined point and counted disagreements with the vector join.
  6. Point rasters. Rasterized the survey points at 1 km and 30 m with a fixed burn value of 1, and counted lit cells.

What I found

Areas measured planar in EPSG:32651 (WGS 84 / UTM 51N), which departs
from the ellipsoidal area by under 0.03 per cent at this latitude.
Gridded areas were computed by cell count on a 30 m grid with origin
295000, 3528000; shifting that origin by half a cell moves a small
district by several per cent, so gridded figures are reported to no
better than the nearest per cent.

Raster queries are faster (constant-time) but less accurate near boundaries. The 30 m raster matches 2,794 of 2,795 points; 100 m matches 2,789. Vector queries are slower but more precise.

8. What the vector layer was hiding

The vector layer itself has ~600 m resolution (mean vertex spacing), despite 11 cm coordinate precision. High-resolution rasters waste storage on false precision.

What this does not show

  • The 553 missing respondents. They are simply not on the map. Every rate computed from the 2,795-point layer is a rate over 83.5% of the survey, and nobody checking the layer for nulls would notice.
  • Area under a sloped or rough surface. Planimetric area is the horizontal shadow. If the ground is not flat, the surface area is larger.
